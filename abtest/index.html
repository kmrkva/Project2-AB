Designing Your A/B Test Survey

Your test will typically be one choice between your client's product and the competitor. Half of participants will see your original product image (control/Version A) and half will see your redesign (Version B). Everything else stays the same including the competitor product, so if people are more likely to choose your product in Version B than in Version A, it must have been caused by your redesign.

Everyone's choices from class will eventually be combined into one study and shown to participants in a random order. The participants will be some combination of US adults from an online survey panel (Prolific) and people who you send the survey to (e.g., friends, family). Fill in each step below, check the live preview, then download your file. Test it a few times to confirm that you get both versions and that it looks good. When you're happy with it, submit it on Canvas.

2. The products

3. Your three images

Use just the product image for each, not a screenshot of the whole web page. Brand name, price and other details are added below the image automatically. All three images are shown in identical boxes, so exact pixel sizes don't matter.

Keep it well-controlled. Your original and redesign should differ only in what your redesign is meant to change. Any other difference could also explain your results. Check each box before uploading:

Images aren't saved if you leave this page, so keep your files handy until you've downloaded.

4. Prices

We recommend no prices or the same price for both. A price difference can easily outweigh your redesign.

5. The question

Shown above the two products. Any wording is fine; keep it short.

6. Image display

The box behind each product image. White suits nearly all retail product photos. Pick black only if your images have dark backgrounds.

Both products always get the same box, so they appear the same size. Large or detailed images are fine; they're scaled to fit.

7. Extra listing details

By default each product shows only its image, brand, optional price and "Free Shipping · 30-Day Free Returns". No stars, reviews or badges.

8. Helpful demographics

Which would be useful for analyzing your results? Check all that apply. These are not added to your choice; they help decide a short set of questions everyone gets at the start or end. Gender and age will almost certainly be included, plus possibly 1–3 others.

9. Follow-up questions

9a. Does it work for likely buyers?

Optional question shown right below the two products. Answers go in your id_r column, so you can check whether your redesign works among people who would actually buy this kind of product.

9b and 9c are not added to your choice. If you can measure something with one simple question, write it in; we might add it to the start or end of the study.
